Best places in the window

Puget Sound Chinook, against the guideline

How much of each quota has been used. These fisheries are managed on encounters rather than on catch, so the figure counted against the guideline is encounters or harvest, whichever WDFW set for that area. An area can run two guideline fisheries in a season, so each is listed under the day it opened. Only areas with a quota appear — this is a measure of quota used, not a statement about whether water is open, which is set by the sport fishing rules.

WDFW state the date each estimate runs through, and that date, not today's, is how current the figure is.

Ocean salmon, by port

Chinook run against a guideline and coho against a quota, and they run out at different times: a port can be most of the way through its Chinook and barely into its coho. Where WDFW publish the share used but not the ceiling, the ceiling is worked back out of their own two figures and rounded, so it is an implied number rather than a published one.

What is not read

One entry on WDFW's creel index carries no in-season data: Sport catch reports, the annual catch-record-card publications, whose most recent edition covers 2021. They summarise a year rather than track a season, so nothing on this page is built from them. Everything else WDFW lists there is read, including the two quota trackers on the previous tab.

How current each source is

The date of the most recent report read from each source, and how long ago that was. A source that runs a season — Buoy 10 opens in August, Willapa in August, the pikeminnow fishery in spring — is not stale between seasons; it has simply not published yet.

How a number gets here

Every figure is parsed from a WDFW report and nothing is estimated. Catch and effort are kept in separate tables, because one interview counts anglers once and their fish once per species; folding them together would inflate every catch-per-angler figure. Every place that reported fishing is shown, but a rate resting on fewer than anglers is marked thin, drawn faded, and never ranked in the heating and cooling lists — one lucky boat moves a six-angler rate by half. Where a report records whether a fish was fin-clipped, that split is carried too: hatchery and wild are not the same fish to a biologist, and under mark-selective rules they are not the same fish to an angler either.
